MyFriends Advent

4 weeks to experience Christmas with friends.

December is already packed with dinners, coffees, and catch-ups. Instead of adding more events to your calendar, use the conversations that are already going to happen. This simple guide helps you listen, share your experiences, pray for friends, and discover the Christmas story with them.

Preparation

Read me before starting

The essentials to make the most of this journey throughout the month.

+

This journey wasn't designed for one-off conversations with strangers, but to be lived with continuity over four weeks. Consistency builds trust: when you listen in Week 1, share your experience in Week 2, and pray for their struggles in Week 3, opening the Christmas story together in Week 4 becomes a natural step.

2 to 4 friends for this journey

Don't bet everything on just one person. In December, schedules fail, coffees get postponed, and not every conversation flows the first time. Think of 2 to 4 friends who are already part of your weekly routine and try taking these steps with all of them. Naturally, with one or two, the conversation will gain depth:

  • A parent you talk to at the school gates
  • A relative you see every weekend
  • The barista at your local coffee shop
  • A coworker, someone from the gym, or a neighbor

Pause today and ask the Holy Spirit who are the people He is already preparing for you to bless during this season.

Week 1

Listen and Learn

Ask good questions over coffee or dinner and listen to your friend's story without rushing.

+

This week, the challenge is simple: over coffee, dinner, or a quick chat, ask good questions and listen with genuine attention, with no rush to give answers.

To start the conversation:
  • What do you like most about Christmas?
  • What do you like least?
To go a little deeper:
  • How did your background or your family shape the way you see this season?
  • What is your fondest Christmas memory?
  • If you could change one thing about Christmas today to make it truly meaningful for you, what would it be?
Week 2

The Story and Your Experience

Read a Christmas passage alone and then share your recent experience with Jesus.

+

Choose one of these two passages about Christmas to read and reflect on this week:

  • Option A - Luke 2:8-20 (God appears to ordinary workers in the middle of the night)
  • Option B - Matthew 1:18-25 (Joseph deals with shattered plans and learns to trust)
Reflect on your own first:
  • What stands out to me most in this story today? What is God highlighting for me to consider?
  • How does this connect with a recent experience I've had with Jesus?
This week's step:

In a natural conversation with your friend, briefly tell them this Christmas story and share for 1 or 2 minutes what you have recently experienced with Jesus because of it. Share your experience with Jesus, rather than just information about Jesus.

Week 3

Brokenness and Prayer

Talk about the challenges of this season and say a quick prayer for your friend.

+

Jesus wasn't born in a palace, but in the vulnerability of a stable. It is right in our brokenness that He comes to meet us.

Ask your friend:
  • What has been most challenging for you lately?
  • What is the hardest part about this time of year for you?

Reflect: Think about your own answers to these questions and how Jesus has met you in your own brokenness.

This week's step:

Offer to pray for the specific brokenness your friend shared. Keep the prayer simple and short (15 to 30 seconds), with your eyes open and using normal everyday language.

How to suggest it: "I know this might seem a bit different, but I usually ask Jesus for help with the hard stuff. Would you mind if I said a quick prayer for you right now, right here?"

Week 4

The Story of Jesus

Read the Christmas story together and discover what steps of change you can take.

+

Go back to the same passage you chose in Week 2 (Luke 2:8-20 or Matthew 1:18-25). Tell this story to your friend or read the text together.

Discuss these questions together:
  • What stands out to you the most in this story?
  • What does it say about God and about people?
  • If this story is really true, what is it inviting you to change in your life?
  • What is the first simple step you can take next week in that direction?
  • Who did this story remind you of, and who could you share it with? (Or: Who can you share this with to help you take that first step?)
